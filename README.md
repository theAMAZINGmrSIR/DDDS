DOOMSDAY DELIVERY SERVICE

A satirical, B-horror-inspired third-person action game about corporate loyalty, disposable workers, and the end of the world.

 Overview

Doomsday Delivery Service (DDDS) is a darkly comedic third-person action game set in a corporate dystopia where apocalyptic events are just another business opportunity.
You play as an Asset Manager, remotely controlling 3D-printed “Employees” via a surveillance drone. These Employees are disposable. The deliveries are mandatory. The world is ending.
And productivity must increase.

 Core Gameplay Loop

Select Mission – Choose a delivery job in an unstable, apocalyptic zone.
Print Employee – Use collected bio-matter to 3D-print a new worker.
Maintain Drone Link – Keep connection stable or risk malfunction/escape.
Retrieve & Deliver Package – Navigate hazards, enemies, and environmental chaos.
Employee Death? – Print another. Packages must arrive.

 Vision

 A Parody of the Gig Economy

Doomsday Delivery Service is a satirical reflection of modern gig-economy culture — where workers are treated as replaceable, metrics matter more than people, and efficiency is valued above humanity.

In DDDS:

Employees are literally disposable.
Productivity is tracked.
Death is a minor inconvenience.
The system reassures you that “everything is functioning normally.”
The exaggerated dystopia mirrors real-world systems where delivery drivers, warehouse workers, and gig contractors are pushed to optimize performance while operating under surveillance, unstable conditions, and algorithmic management.
The horror in DDDS isn’t just monsters — it’s normalization of exploitation.

 Corporate Convenience at Any Cost

The game draws inspiration from companies like Amazon and DoorDash — not to attack individuals, but to parody the systems that prioritize:
Speed over safety
Metrics over wellbeing
Convenience over consequence
In DDDS, apocalyptic disasters are business opportunities. Catastrophe increases demand. The world ending simply means “higher quarterly performance.”
By turning delivery drivers into 3D-printed corporate assets, the game exaggerates the dehumanization already present in parts of modern logistics culture.

It asks:

What happens when workers are reduced to data?
What happens when automation replaces empathy?
What happens when efficiency becomes morality?

DDDS is designed as:

A satire of gig-economy exploitation
A parody of corporate dystopia
A love letter to B-horror cinema
A mechanically tight third-person action game

No loot boxes.
No predatory monetization.
Just survival, satire, and deliveries.
